World clock

The strip above, for every admin key: one dial per region, ranked by offset around the reference zone. Up to 12 regions, plus the reference. Applies immediately.

How the dials read

Each region gets a dial, its time and its name, ranked by offset — earliest on the left, latest on the right — around the reference below, which therefore sits in the middle. The face of each dial is that city’s actual sky, shaded from the sun’s height above its horizon: the zenith at the top, the horizon at the bottom, so the warmth of a sunset stays low on the dial while the top has already turned to night. There are no steps — the colour moves with the sun, and follows the season and the latitude, so a northern winter afternoon reads as dusk while the tropics are still in daylight. The time turns green between 18:00 and 01:00 locally, the hours when players come to the tables. A weekday is appended where the region has already rolled over, and hovering a dial gives its phase and the sun’s altitude. Up to 12 regions, plus the reference, which is always shown whether or not it is ticked. This server’s own clock is not here — it heads the status list under Health & logs. Applies immediately (no restart needed).

pokerth.net vs web client

Every 5 minutes the proxy reads the official counters of pokerth.net (the same figures as the Internet card of the login screen: online, tables, waiting, today) and counts, at that same instant, the web client players signed in to pokerth.net — through this proxy or connected straight to it. Share = web client players ÷ players online on pokerth.net, from hourly means (48 h) or daily means (period). /live spectators are shown apart, not in the share. Recording started with 2.1.10-web.9 and needs the live figures switched on (Settings): hours before that are empty, not quiet.

Where visitors come from

One source per visit, read from the landing page before the address bar is cleaned: a utm_source tag if the link had one, else the domain of the referring site (never its path), else a table invite link, else one of this site’s own pages, else a ?lang= link, else direct — typed, bookmark, installed app, or a referrer withheld by the browser or the app the link was opened in (chat apps usually withhold it). Sources are then folded into channels for reading: search engines, AI assistants (ChatGPT tags its own links utm_source=chatgpt.com, so that is not a campaign of ours), PokerTH.net, social and chat apps, real campaigns, other sites; a channel with several sources unfolds. “New” = first visits; returning = every other visit. Channels = visits by channel; New visitors = the same channels ranked by first visits, against returning ones; Languages = first visits by source and browser language (languages are kept for first visits only). Follows the period selected above.

World map

Where visits come from, by country, over the last 14 days. The country is read from the browser’s time zone (Europe/Paris → France), sent with the same anonymous visit ping — no IP address is looked up. It is an approximation: a VPN does not change it, a traveller’s device does, and zones the table does not know (UTC, Etc/GMT…) count as Unknown. “Visits” counts browser sessions, “New devices” the devices seen for the first time, “Installed app” and “Phones” the visits from the installed app and from a phone or tablet; “Connections” counts the sessions that opened a game, in the entry modes picked at the top of the page, as in Activity › Where sessions open — a grey bar in the list means visits that never opened a game. The band above the map gives the local time along it, the evening in green. Detected bots are left out while “Bots hidden” is on; a country that still looks automated (only new devices, no game) is tagged “bot?”. “Day / night” shades where it is night right now, with the civil, nautical and astronomical twilights, from the real position of the sun — season and hemisphere included; the country card then adds its local time (legal time, summer time included; the main zone for a country on several) and its sunset, at the centre of the country. Counted since 2.1.10-web.28: older clients send no time zone and are left out, so totals here can be below the Overview’s.

Client script errors

JavaScript errors reported by players' browsers, one line per bug with a counter. Kept in memory only — a restart clears the list. Copy all and Export take the errors the list shows, filters included.

What is sent, bots, and the colours

Players can switch the reporting off in Advanced options; nothing but the error itself, the build, the device and a masked IP is ever sent. Errors from search-engine crawlers are kept but marked Bot only (or incl. bots): none of them reaches a player. Each error carries a colour by type: script error (an exception in the client), rejection (a promise nobody caught), load failure (one of our files did not arrive), recovered (an automatic retry worked) and diagnostic (the probe that tells why a file failed).

Search engine visibility (SEO)

Off by default: pages say noindex and robots.txt blocks every crawler, so a self-hosted install stays out of Google and AI search engines. Turn it on only for a public instance you want indexed. Applies immediately.

What indexing adds

The page gains a description, Open Graph / Twitter cards, a canonical link, structured data (JSON-LD) and a crawler-readable summary, and robots.txt, sitemap.xml and llms.txt are generated from the public URL below.

Running the official instance? Fill in pokerth.net settings puts every field back to what webclient.pokerth.net uses. Nothing is written until you press Save.

What the preset sets

Indexing on, the public URL, PokerTH as the site name, AI crawlers allowed, and the branding overrides left empty so each language keeps its own translated title and description. Your Search Console, Bing and Yandex tokens are not touched — they belong to your accounts, not to the preset.

Roll back a deployment

        
Every state this server has served, newest first. Static swaps the files without dropping anyone; with restart is what a change to proxy.js needs.

        
How a rollback works
Rolling back checks the chosen commit out again — the quickest way out when a deploy breaks the client. With restart also reinstalls dependencies, then restarts. The state you are on now is recorded first, so you can always come back forward. With Update automatically on, the version you stepped back from is left alone until a newer one is published (a fix, typically); an update made by hand goes forward at once.

Pending requests 

          

        
Servers proposed from the login page (LAN › Propose my server). Approving adds one to every player’s LAN list and to the dial allowlist; the player gets no answer — the server appearing in the list is the answer.

        
What is kept, and what players see
The game-server registry is not touched. What the requester wrote for the admins (nickname, note, contact) is never shown to players; the contact is erased as soon as you approve or reject, the note becomes the server’s internal note. No IP is kept (only a salted hash, to count requests from one address over 30 days).

Settings

        

          
          
          
          
        

        
Turn “Refuse private addresses” off only on a self-hosted LAN install. What the login page’s LAN button lists and pre-fills (offline servers, “Local server”, manual entry, the default server) is set in Infrastructure › Game servers.

        
Private addresses, Local server and manual entry
Refusing private addresses only makes sense off when this proxy runs on the same network as the servers. “Local server” is the address the LAN form used before the directory (the default login form, else this page’s host on port 7234): keep it on a self-hosted install, turn it off on pokerth.net. Manual entry lets a player type an address instead of picking one; addresses outside the allowlist are still refused.
